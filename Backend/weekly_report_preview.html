3. Potential Risks
The primary potential risk is the sheer volume of risk alerts without corresponding notifications. While it’s positive that risks are being identified, a consistently high number of alerts could indicate:
* Overly sensitive alert thresholds, leading to "alert fatigue" if many are false positives.
* Latent issues that are repeatedly triggering alerts but not yet manifesting as critical problems.
* A disconnect between alert generation and the subsequent resolution or notification process.

4. Recommendations For Management
* Investigate the nature of the 45 risk alerts. Categorize them by type, severity, and recurrence to understand their underlying causes.
* Review current alert thresholds and tuning. Determine if some alerts are consistently false positives and can be refined to reduce noise while maintaining effective detection.
* Assess the workflow for risk alert resolution. Ensure there's a clear process for investigating, mitigating, and documenting each alert, even if no user notification is required.
* Consider implementing a tracking mechanism for alert resolution times and outcomes to identify areas for process improvement.
* Monitor the trend of "Notifications Sent" in relation to "Risk Alerts Generated." A sustained zero notification count with high alerts needs careful scrutiny to ensure risks are genuinely being managed and not just ignored.
